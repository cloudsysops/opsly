# Messenger

**Character data:** [`../characters/messenger.json`](../characters/messenger.json)
**Merged in from:** `feat/icso-youtube-kids-swim` — see [`CONTINUITY-RULES.md`](CONTINUITY-RULES.md).

A mythic, non-human entity that opens questions rather than answers.
Appears from and dissolves back into AETHER (see [`WORLDS.md`](WORLDS.md)),
visually tied to the same sacred-geometry family as the cipher fragments
(see [`SYMBOLS.md`](SYMBOLS.md)).

## Function

Turning-point device: the Messenger's brief appearances mark moments where
a question opens rather than resolves — never a source of complete
answers. Never has a fixed humanoid body across appearances, never speaks
with an audible voice (communicates only through brief symbol/text
fragments).

## The rule

> Los Mensajeros pueden mostrar una puerta. Jamás atravesarla por ti.
> (Messengers can show a door. They never walk through it for you.)

A Messenger never resolves a decision on-screen — it appears at a genuine
turning point, leaves a question or a single symbol, and dissolves.
Preserves free will as a theme: the character (and the audience) still has
to choose.

## Named individual messengers

Multiple messengers can appear across the universe rather than one
recurring figure — same design language (`messenger.json`), different
names/contexts:

- **Ariel** — a working name for a messenger appearing at moments of
  creative risk.
- **Lumiel** — a working name for a messenger appearing at moments of
  loss/failure (pairs naturally with the luminous-ruins device in
  `SYMBOLS.md`).
- **El Guardián** — a working name for a messenger appearing at moments of
  protection/safety (a natural fit for Peskids/child-safety content).

These are naming conventions for *how a Messenger presents itself in a
given scene*, not three separate characters — all draw on the single
`messenger.json` CharacterProfile. Don't create separate character JSON
files for them unless a specific season needs a genuinely distinct visual
design for one.
